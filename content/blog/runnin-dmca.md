[Gaia](/music/projects/gaia_marenghi) and I released a cover of Runnin' (Lose It
All) in August. On the 3rd of October, it disappeared from all streaming
services.

<!--more-->

At this point the only thing we knew was that the release vanished from the
account and from streaming services. To start investigating this, we first
needed send some messages to the automated user support thing and convince it to
send our request to a human. We included all the details, with some problems,
like we couldn't look up the UPC for the release, because that number is in the
release entry in the distributor side, and that's the very entry that vanished.
In any case, we got a response:

> Hello,
> 
> Thank you for reaching out.
> 
> One or more of your releases was removed in response to a claim from a rights
> owner that it is infringing that rights owners’ copyrighted sound recordings.
> This concerns the following release(s):
> 
> “Runnin'” (UPC 825373805058)
>
> A copyright strike has been applied to your account as a result of this
> takedown.
> 
> We understand that receiving a message like this may be upsetting, but we take
> the protection of copyright seriously, and are continually investing in
> processes to protect artists and their music.
> 
> Best,

This looks like someone considered this an infrigement to a sound recording.
This would be the derivative work case, rather than a missing mechanical license
case.

The biggest issue was the copyright strike applied to the account. We had to
fight this. So we sent a longer email detailing out why we think this is a
misunderstanding. We included the number of the mechanical license, described
how the recording was produced (all parts played or manually programmed by us,
no samples used), and offering DAW screenshots and unmixed stems. 

We also requested the name of the claimant, because their email didn't even
specify who that was. This seems asymmetrical to me. Our work has been taken
down without a warning or even a notification, because of a claim from an
unknown source.

> Hello,
> 
> I am appealing the takedown of "Runnin'" (UPC 825373805058) and the copyright
> strike applied to my account, as described in your message of 6 October 2026.
> The claim is mistaken.
> 
> What the release is
> 
> “Runnin’ (Lose It All)" is my cover of the song written by Beyoncé, Arrow
> Benjamin and Naughty Boy. The recording is an original performance. No portion
> of the Beyonce’/Naughty Boy recording, or of any other commercially released
> recording, was used. Every part was performed or programmed from scratch by
> the three artists credited on the release: Maciej Blizinski, Riccardo
> Marenghi, and myself. The only material taken from the original is the
> composition, meaning the melody and lyrics.
> 
> I purchased a DistroKid cover license for this specific release on the 20th of
> April, license ID is 102354408. The release went live on 25 August 2026.
> DistroKid's "Confirmation of License to Make and Distribute Digital
> Phonorecords" is attached as license_102354408.pdf. It lists:
> 
> - Title: Runnin'
> - Authors: Beyoncé, Arrow Benjamin, Naughty Boy
> - Release date: 25 August 2026
> - Length: 3:19
> - Configuration: digital phonorecord delivery
> - Licensee: Gaia Marenghi
>
> The license covers the musical composition. The claim concerns a sound
> recording. I did not use the original sound recording.
> 
> The release was live on DistroKid and on streaming services from 25 August
> 2026 without any issue. On the 3rd of October 2026 it was removed from
> DistroKid and from all stores, and the release entry vanished from my account.
> I received no notice from DistroKid. On the 4rd of October 2026 I received an
> email from Spotify's monitoring service, which gave no reason. I am forwarding
> it with this message.
> 
> I can send dated DAW session screenshots, stems and multitracks, and the
> performer credits for all three artists.
> 
> I am asking for the following:
> 
> - The claimant's name, the recording they say is infringed, how the match was
>   made (automated fingerprint or manual report), and a copy of the claim.
> - Rescission of the copyright strike and written confirmation of my account's
>   standing.
> - Reinstatement of the release under the same UPC (825373805058) and ISRC,
>   with redelivery to all stores.
> - Restoration of the release record in my account, so I can see the release
>   history.
>
> Maciej Bliziński (cc) is authorized to correspond on my behalf about this
> request.
>
> Regards,
> Gaia Marenghi

A few days later the response came:

> Hi Gaia,
>
> After a further review, "Runnin'" has been restored to the stores. There is
> nothing you need to change on your end.
>
> It can take a few days for the stores to show the release again. If it is
> still missing after a week, reply here and we will check on it.
>
> Best,

At the time of writing the release is not yet back online. Keeping fingers
crossed, hopefully there really isn't anything else to do for now. But the
incident raises some questions:

Why did we never learn who was making the claim?

Why was our fully original audio recording mistaken for somebody else's
recording?

Why did we only learn about the copyright strike after filing a support request?

Can any of our original recordings be considered an infringement?

Our case was a mild one, but the incident shows worrying patterns. Release taken
down with no notification or information on really what happened. If we wanted
to submit a DMCA counterclaim, we wouldn't know against whom to submit it.
